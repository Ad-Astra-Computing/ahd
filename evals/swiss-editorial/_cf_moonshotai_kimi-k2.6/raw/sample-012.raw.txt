            <h1>A design evaluation framework<br>that treats <em>slop</em> as a bug, not a speedrun.</h1>
        </header>

        <section>
            <p>You have already seen the problem. Mid-fidelity screens that share the same curvature, the same three-column hero, the same illustration of a person whose fingers dissolve into the background. The tools are fast. The output is seductively complete. And the work is interchangeable.</p>
            <p>AHD is not a design tool. It is a framework for evaluating whether a design output—generated, hand-drawn, or mixed—meets <strong>human</strong> standards for coherence, constraint, and intention. You write a brief. The compiler turns it into an evaluation specification. A battery of linters, critic models, and comparative harnesses run against the output. You get a report with failures ranked by severity, not a star rating.</p>
            <p>The entire pipeline is local-first. You own the spec, the rules, and the threshold for what counts as passing.</p>

            <p>The command above compiles a brief, runs it against ten model outputs, and produces a structured report: rule violations, visual deltas, and a scored comparison matrix. The <code>--models &lt;specs&gt;</code> flag accepts anything from local ONNX weights to frontier APIs. The harness is model-agnostic by design.</p>
