          <p class="eyebrow">Framework, not sermon</p>
          <h1 id="hero-title">Design work should survive contact with taste.</h1>
          <p class="lede">
            AHD is for people who can already spot generated sameness on sight. It does not explain why slop is a problem. It gives you a brief compiler, a rule set, an eval harness, and enough structure to reject the obvious failure modes before they land in production.
          </p>
          <div class="hero-notes">
            <div class="note">Not a gallery of prompts. Not a vibes-only rubric. It is an opinionated test rig for visual and interface quality.</div>
            <div class="note">One screen, one command, one manifest. The question is whether the work holds up when inspected.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <aside class="terminal" aria-label="AHD eval command">
          <div class="terminal-inner">
            <p class="terminal-label">Run the eval</p>
            <pre><code class="language-bash">ahd eval-live swiss-editorial --brief briefs/landing.yml --models &lt;specs&gt; --n 10</code></pre>
            <p class="side-copy">
              The point is not to generate a page and declare victory. The point is to compare outputs against a concrete brief, inspect the misses, and keep the taste boundary explicit.
            </p>
