        <div class="hero-copy">
          <p class="eyebrow">Against generic output</p>
          <h1 id="hero-title">AHD turns taste into constraints you can inspect.</h1>
          <p class="lede">The problem is not that models can produce layouts. The problem is that most teams have no serious mechanism for checking whether those layouts obey a brief, preserve intent, or collapse into the same visual habits.</p>
          <p class="subcopy">AHD gives designers, engineers, and founders a working frame: compile the brief, lint the output, run the eval, and keep a manifest of what is actually operational versus what still depends on external services.</p>
          <div class="cta-row">
            <a class="cta-link" href="https://github.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Read the code</a>
            <span class="mini-note">One screen, one command, no theatre.</span>
          </div>
        </div>

      <section class="closing" aria-label="Closing statement">
        <p>AHD is not a sermon about bad output. It is a stricter working loop for teams that want to state their standards in plain language and test against them.</p>
        <p>The point is simple: if a system claims to help with design, it should tolerate scrutiny at the level of the brief, the rules, and the artifact.</p>
