          <p class="kicker">Framework, not sermon</p>
          <h1 id="hero-title">The point is to catch the slop before it ships.</h1>
          <p class="hero-lead">
            AHD is a working discipline for teams that already know the failure mode: generic output, weak taste, vague prompts, no audit trail. It turns the critique into a repeatable system: compile the brief, lint the output, run the eval, inspect what failed, and keep the constraints visible.
          </p>
          <div class="hero-meta" aria-label="Summary">
            <div class="meta-block">
              <span class="meta-label">For</span>
              <p>Designers, engineers, and founders who want a standard they can defend in review.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="meta-block">
              <span class="meta-label">Method</span>
              <p>Rules, manifests, tokens, and evals that can be read, argued with, and improved.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

        <div>
          <p class="statement" id="statement-title">
            Most AI site generation fails for boring reasons: the brief is under-specified, the design language is mush, and nobody can say what “good” meant after the screenshots start circulating. AHD treats those as tractable engineering problems. The output can still surprise you. The standard cannot.
          </p>
        </div>

        <section class="manifest" id="what-ships" aria-labelledby="ships-title">
          <h2 id="ships-title">What ships</h2>
          <p>Available now in the repo and intended to be used together.</p>
          <ul>
            <li><span>Brief compiler</span><span>Turns intent into a structured input the system can actually enforce</span></li>
            <li><span>28-rule linter</span><span>Flags the recurring visual and structural failure modes</span></li>
            <li><span>Eval harness</span><span>Runs comparative passes and keeps the result inspectable</span></li>
            <li><span>Vision-critic scaffold</span><span>Skeleton for image-aware review and failure tagging</span></li>
            <li><span>MCP server</span><span>Connects the workflow into agent and editor environments</span></li>
            <li><span>Editor plugins</span><span>In-repo integrations for the working loop</span></li>
            <li><span>Eight tokens</span><span>Eight opinionated design languages to evaluate against</span></li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section class="manifest" id="what-is-gated" aria-labelledby="gated-title">
          <h2 id="gated-title">What is gated</h2>
          <p>These are intentionally separated because they depend on external services or packaging work.</p>
          <ul>
            <li><span>Live frontier-model calls</span><span>Needs API keys</span></li>
            <li><span>Live vision critique</span><span>Needs multimodal key + screenshot pipeline</span></li>
            <li><span>Standalone npm packages for the editor plugins</span><span>Requires packaging and publication outside the core repo</span></li>
            <li><span>Additional tokens</span><span>Shipped after they meet the same constraint and eval standard</span></li>
          </ul>
