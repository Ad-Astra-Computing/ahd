Framework, not sermon

AHD treats generated design like something that can fail inspection.

Artificial Human Design is for people who already know what generated slop looks like. The point is not to repeat the diagnosis. The point is to make taste, restraint, and implementation discipline testable enough to work inside a real workflow.

For designers, it keeps the brief and the visual rules in the same conversation.

For engineers, it turns hand-wavy quality complaints into an eval surface.

For founders, it shows what can be run today and what still depends on external model access.

AHD is opinionated by design. It assumes the brief matters, that visual language can be named precisely, and that “looks generated” is a tractable failure mode rather than an unavoidable side effect.

The output is intentionally plain about scope. No mythology, no filler sections, no decorative proof signals. Just the toolchain, the command, and the current boundary between local capability and external dependency.
