            <p class="eyebrow">Framework, not sermon</p>
            <h1>Design work should survive inspection.</h1>
            <p class="lede">AHD exists for teams who can already spot generated sameness on sight and want a repeatable way to reject it before it reaches production.</p>
            <p>Not another gallery of polished guesses. <strong>AHD turns taste into a test surface</strong>: a brief compiler, a linter that names the failure mode, an eval harness that runs multiple attempts, and the scaffolding needed to compare what the model made against what the brief actually asked for.</p>
            <p>The point is not to moralize about slop. The point is to give designers, engineers, and founders a shared apparatus for saying why something fails, where it fails, and whether the system can be pushed into better behavior.</p>
            <div class="cta-row">
              <a class="cta" href="https://github.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Read the code</a>
            </div>
          </div>

          <aside class="aside" aria-label="Context">
            <section class="note">
              <h2>Operating stance</h2>
              <p>AHD is opinionated by design. It assumes the brief matters, visual taste can be articulated, and evaluation should be inspectable rather than hidden behind a vibe score.</p>
            </section>
            <section class="note">
              <h2>One screen</h2>

This project is an attempt to make that problem concrete enough to work on.</p>
