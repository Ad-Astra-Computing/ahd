  <section class="hero">
    <div class="meta">v0.4 · public working copy · 2026-04-24</div>
    <h1>A framework for design that <em>survives</em> being generated by a language model.</h1>
    <p class="dek">You have already identified the problem. Another month of soft drop shadows, rounded rectangles in tidy rows, marketing copy that forgets what it is selling. AHD does not argue about it. It compiles a brief, lints the output against 28 rules lifted from actual editorial practice, and fails loudly when a model produces the thing you are tired of looking at.</p>
    <p class="lede">Everything below is either in the repository today or explicitly gated on something you would have to plug in yourself. The distinction is the whole point.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="run">
    <h2><span class="num">01</span>Run the eval</h2>
    <p>One command. It takes a brief, runs N generations across the model specs you name, lints them, scores them, and writes a report you can diff against the previous run.</p>
    <div class="term" role="group" aria-label="terminal">
      <div class="bar"><span>~/ahd — zsh</span><span class="dots">● ● ●</span></div>
<pre><code class="language-bash"><span class="prompt">$ </span><span class="cmd">ahd eval-live swiss-editorial</span> <span class="flag">--brief</span> <span class="arg">briefs/landing.yml</span> <span class="flag">--models</span> <span class="arg">&lt;specs&gt;</span> <span class="flag">--n</span> <span class="arg">10</span>
<span class="out">→ compiling brief … ok (14 constraints, 8 tokens loaded)
→ dispatching 10 generations across specs …
→ lint pass: 28/28 rules evaluated
→ vision-critic: scaffold ready (supply --vision-key to activate)
→ report: reports/2026-04-24T09-12Z/index.html</span></code></pre>
    </div>
    <div class="caption">language: bash · no telemetry · output is a static report, not a dashboard</div>
  </section>

  <section id="stance" class="stance">
    <h2><span class="num">02</span>Stance</h2>
    <blockquote class="pull">The problem is not that models generate. The problem is that nothing downstream of the prompt is willing to reject what they produce.</blockquote>
    <p>AHD is that downstream. A brief compiler that refuses ambiguous inputs. A linter with opinions about measure, hierarchy, and rhythm. An eval harness that treats a generation as a hypothesis rather than a deliverable. It is boring on purpose. It is also the part nobody was writing.</p>
  </section>
